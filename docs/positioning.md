# Discuno Positioning and Public Voice

This is the source of truth for public-facing product copy. Legal, support, and checkout copy may
be more explicit, but acquisition copy should follow this hierarchy.

## Position

**Category:** student-to-student guidance for college decisions.

**Primary audience:** current college students deciding on courses, majors, internships,
recruiting, campus opportunities, graduate school, or a change in direction.

**Core promise:** talk through a real decision with someone who has relevant firsthand context.

**Desired outcome:** a clearer next move, fewer unknowns, and better questions. Never promise an
admission, job, grade, placement, or other guaranteed result.

**Difference:** Discuno is not generic coaching or another library of advice. It helps a student
find someone a few steps ahead whose school, field, or lived experience overlaps with the decision
in front of them.

## Message Hierarchy

1. Start with the decision, tension, or question the student already feels.
2. Show how relevant firsthand experience changes that moment.
3. Put real mentors and the questions they can help with ahead of product mechanics.
4. Use proof only when it is accurate and attributable.
5. Explain scheduling, payment, accounts, and policies only where they remove friction.

## Voice

- Specific, human, calm, and candid.
- Write to one student with one decision—not to a generic “community.”
- Prefer concrete questions over abstract claims about growth or empowerment.
- Sound like a thoughtful student a few steps ahead, never an institution or motivational poster.
- Use short sentences and contractions when they make the copy feel natural.

## Language Standards

- Use **conversation** in persuasion copy and **session** in transactional copy.
- Use **student** or **someone looking for guidance**, not **mentee**, outside legal or internal
  contexts.
- Discovery CTA: **Find a mentor** or **Find someone who's been there**.
- Mentor-card CTA: **See how they can help** or **See [name]'s sessions**.
- Profile CTA: **See available times**.
- Supply CTA: **Start mentoring**.
- Avoid **platform**, **feature**, **integration**, **powered by**, and vendor names in persuasion
  copy. Cal.com and Stripe belong in support, legal, and the relevant transaction step.

## Trust Boundaries

- Say **school email confirmed** only when the school-email flag is true. “Confirmed” describes
  access to the address without implying that Discuno verified the person more broadly.
- Explain that the check confirms access to a supported institutional email address and supports a
  stated affiliation. It is not identity, background, expertise, credential, or outcome
  verification.
- Do not call mentors, advice, or the community “verified” more broadly.
- Use testimonials only from real completed sessions with permission. Do not invent or composite
  reviews.
- Peer perspective complements official academic, financial, legal, and medical guidance.

## Page Pattern

For acquisition pages, prefer this order:

1. Decision-led promise
2. Real people or decision-led discovery
3. Concrete questions and use cases
4. Genuine proof, once available
5. A short explanation of the conversation
6. Compact trust and policy reassurance
7. One clear call to action

## Near-Term Growth Loop

Do not buy broad traffic before the marketplace has real proof. Start with one mentor-dense school,
major, or recruiting use case; curate 5–10 strong profiles; manually support 15–25 sessions; and
capture the student's question, post-session clarity, next action, and permission to quote. Use
those genuine signals to choose the next positioning and acquisition experiment.
